How a forecast is made and how the tool is judged. Six parts: three rules that bind everything, then evidence, models, analysts, your input, and the yardstick.

1. Three rules that bind everything

  1. Judge the process, never tune on outcomes. Accuracy is recorded after each decision and never used to adjust anything. With eight decisions a year, tuning on results would be fitting noise.
  2. Pre-register, then amend in the open. The scorecard, the pool weights, the surprise floor, every model rule that can move the number and every fitted model's specification are written down before use. Any change is a numbered amendment with its reason; a hash of the spec is pinned in the tests, so a silent change fails the build and entries recorded under an earlier spec are labelled.
  3. Record and verify. Every forecast goes into an append-only, hash-chained ledger and must give back its published numbers when recomputed from its snapshot; every analyst run is stored with its prompt hash and scores; everything is committed to git.

2. Evidence: one frozen snapshot

Each pull is one fingerprinted bundle, dated in US time: the meeting calendar, the overnight rate, fed-funds futures, eight FRED series as first published on that day, the last two statements, the minutes, the press-conference transcript, the projections table, speeches since the last decision, 32 years of decisions, and your documents and odds. Nothing outside it may be read: no internet, no memory. Historical fits use data as first published, never revised data. The Evidence graph shows every item and what reads it.

3. Models: code computes the number

  1. Signals are computed, not asked. Inflation gap, labour slack, financial conditions, communication tone, base rates and the committee's projections are plain code over the snapshot. Each lists the items it used and shows its working.
  2. Five models, each with a stated rule. Market-implied (futures price to probabilities), the Fed's four Taylor rules with r* read from the New York Fed DSGE model's natural rate (amendment #7) over a u* grid, your prediction-market odds, historical base rates, and an ordered probit fitted on first-published data. Every rule is in the spec hash.
  3. A fixed, recorded pool. Market 0.6, Taylor 0.2, odds 0.1, historical base rates 0.1 (amendment #6), then a 1.5% floor on every outcome. No language model writes the final number. A model earns a weight only by amendment, after it has beaten the base rate on the expanding-window evaluation and ideally on live decisions; the probit is computed today with weight 0.

4. Analysts: language models read, code checks

  1. Eight analysts, one model. Analysts 1 to 3 are controls (naive; evidence shown, nothing verified; one generalist with the full harness). Analysts 4 to 8 are specialists, each an expert on one slice of the evidence: communications, the minutes, inflation, the labour market, financial conditions. The Analysts and Agents views show what each is asked and given.
  2. The harness. Each analyst sees only its slice of the snapshot plus the numbers code has already computed, with every tool disabled. It answers in a fixed schema: claims, each with a verbatim quote and the item it came from; a report on every watch-out by id; a tilt bounded to ±10 bp. Code verifies every quote, every number and every watch-out, and the run is recorded.
  3. Measured on a ladder. Analysts 1, 2, 3 and 4 to 8 are the same model with one more piece of harness at each step: nothing; the evidence; a fixed answer shape with a quote per claim checked by code; one slice of evidence each. Same snapshot, same scorecard, five runs each, so the change in score at each step belongs to the one thing that was added.
  4. A bounded lean, never a weight. Since amendment #6 the mean of the five specialists' tilts moves the pooled number by the same bounded mechanism as your views: one basis point of tilt is one percentage point of probability, capped at 10. The analysts can lean the number; they cannot write it. The machine track includes this lean; the pool before it is also recorded.

5. Your input: typed channels only

Views tilt the number by a capped amount (3, 6 or 10 pp by strength, 10 pp in total) and expire; the machine-only track is always kept beside the adjusted one. Watch-outs must be answered by id in every analyst run. Documents and prediction-market odds are evidence, frozen into the next snapshot, never a direct nudge. Everything you enter is visible in every forecast it touches.

6. The yardstick: a pre-registered process scorecard

Ten dimensions fixed before any forecasting code existed: repeatability, groundedness, leakage, directional sanity, noise invariance, paraphrase stability, coherence, reasoning quality, auditability, human-input discipline. Eight are measured on every new snapshot, by code; the Scorecard view shows the measurements and what was checked. Outcome accuracy is logged separately and never feeds back.
